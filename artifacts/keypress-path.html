What these trials suggest — and what they do not prove
  • Queueing / scheduling: matched U01 update frames averaged 15.2 s from browser send capture to backend receive in the 10-user capture. This includes scheduling, queues and instrumentation, not just network travel. This is an update-frame population, including metadata; it is not a per-key mean.
  • Backend work: transactions averaged 4.7 ms; synchronous update-listener/broadcast-enqueue spans averaged 16.8 ms. Those spans are not total backend residence time and cannot be added to character latency means.
  • Presence: returning payloads support a forwarding/amplification hypothesis. They do not prove an infinite loop or assign a fraction of latency to awareness traffic. Presence changes are not guaranteed once per key.
  • DOM/style activity: samples contain replacement, geometry checks, styles and GC. Inspected Q8n/e9n include overlay visibility/diagnostic checks; Dca constructs remote caret/selection elements. This does not prove those functions account for all rendering cost.
  • 20-user host: snapshots 52 s apart show ~2.92 GiB swapped in and ~2.60 GiB swapped out. Host counters do not identify ownership of the pages. The run timed out and has incomplete coverage.
  • Scope: an edit, Yjs update, rendered prefix and WebSocket frame are not one-to-one. Input, transport and DOM observation can batch or wait. The diagram groups internal paths where precise per-key causality was not captured.
